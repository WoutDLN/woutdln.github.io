### Description

While the Belgian Association for Anglicists in Higher Education ([BAAHE](https://baahe4.wixsite.com/baahe)) was founded in 1981, it sadly only started to [document conference information and links](https://baahe4.wixsite.com/baahe/about-8) in 2015, the year after the edition I presented at took place. The conference website at the time was hosted on the [University of Antwerp](https://www.uantwerpen.be)'s website, on [a page that has since been removed](https://www.uantwerpen.be/baahe2014). This also means that the conference programme and our abstract has regrettably been lost.

At the conference, I co-presented a talk about the [Beckett Digital Manuscript Project](https://www.beckettarchive.org) (BDMP), together with my then PhD supervisor [Dirk Van Hulle](https://orcid.org/0000-0002-7066-6313) and collague [Vincent Neyt](https://orcid.org/0000-0003-3825-8579). My contribution to this talk presented an abstract visualisation I developed of our interpretation of the sequence in which Samuel Beckett wrote individual sentences in the draft for his novel *L'Innommable* (which he would later translate into English as *The Unnamable*). This visualisation, with a brief explanation of each of its five steps, is freely available on the BDMP website via [https://www.beckettarchive.org/writingsequenceofinnommable](https://www.beckettarchive.org/writingsequenceofinnommable). Dirk later also included a descirption of the visualisation in a 'Work In Progress' contribution to issue 15-16 of *Variants, the Journal of the European Society of Textual Scholarship* (2021), titled ["Dynamic Facsimiles: Note on the Transcription of Born-Digital Works for Genetic Criticism"](https://doi.org/10.4000/variants.1450).
